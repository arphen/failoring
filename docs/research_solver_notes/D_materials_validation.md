Unit bridge ([inf], arithmetic): 1 gf*cm^2/cm = 1 gf*cm = 9.807e-5 N*m. So the current model's bending ~1e-5 N*m is ~0.10 gf*cm^2/cm in KES units. Cantilever (Peirce, 41.5 deg): bending length c ~ l/2 (cos(theta/2)/(8 tan theta) cube-root = 0.5095 at 41.5 deg, my calc), and bending rigidity per width B = w*c^3 with w = areal weight in N/m^2. Example: 100 g/m^2 and B=1e-5 N*m -> c ~ 21.7 mm, overhang l ~ 43 mm. That is a very limp (chiffon/silk-class) fabric; so the "1e-5" preset is chiffon-like, not poplin-like [inf].

## 1. Measurement-to-simulation pipelines: are the parameters/constitutive model enough to reproduce measured drape?

### Takeaway
Yes for static drape of flexible fabrics, no out of the box for stiff ones: the literature shows (a) tensile+shear+bending parameters fitted from standard tests reproduce tensile curves to a few percent (Clyde 2017) and hanging shapes to millimetres (Wang 2011, Larionov 2022), but (b) bending is the weakest link (Clyde's bend-test fit missed the 41.5 deg target by large margins; CLO-virtual drape coefficient deviates increasingly for stiff fabrics), and (c) nobody validates buckling directly (Gong 2026 explicitly excludes it). Bending stiffness is consistently the dominant driver of drape coefficient. The right architecture is: fit tensile/shear from measured curves, fit/verify bending from a cantilever/Cusick-type test through the simulator itself (inverse problem), and regression-test against Cusick DC and node count.

### Cited Findings (pipelines; each with authors, year, venue, link, data/code+licence, failure addressed, accuracy, risk)

Limitations: no plasticity/memory, less accurate at large deformation, no dynamics/damping, compliant materials match worse. Addresses: parameter sufficiency, anisotropic bending. Effort: M (low-cost rig + piecewise-linear fit) [inf]. Licence/code: not found. — [escholarship PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7g35q2x7/qt7g35q2x7.pdf), [SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=114658), [Crossref](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F2010324.1964966)

### Inferences
- [inf] Our parameter set (tensile warp/weft, shear, bending warp/weft, areal density, damping, friction) is the same family as Clyde/CLO/Wang and is sufficient for static drape of limp-to-medium fabrics. Missing vs literature: bias-direction bending (Wang shows bending varies with bias angle), hysteresis/plasticity (needed for persistent wrinkles, Gong 2026), and measured Poisson/coupling term a12 (Clyde had to assume it).
- [inf] The recommended pipeline for a small team: S/M effort tensile+shear fit from curves (Clyde-style) + "bending calibration through the simulator" by matching cantilever overhang and Cusick DC/nodes, with Larionov's FFT-magnitude loss idea if wrinkle fields are compared.
- [inf] Yarn-level approaches (Sperl 2020/2022) are not worth it for woven drape; relevant only if knit J-curve becomes a priority, and even then use their published homogenized stress-strain data as the target curve for the continuum J-curve rather than reimplementing.

## 2. Yarn-level vs continuum, knits (J-curve), hysteresis

### Takeaway
Continuum shell models fitted per fabric are the practical choice; yarn-level and homogenization papers show that knits need nonlinear (strain-stiffening) stress-strain data, and hysteresis/plasticity is only recently being modelled in graphics for wrinkles.

### Cited Findings
- Homogenization (Sperl 2020) builds the shell energy density from many yarn-level runs and reproduces stiffness of wovens and deformability/anisotropy of knits (qualitative). — [ISTA](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/8385)
- Sperl 2022 builds estimation from production-fabric measurements for knits (stiffness, nonlinearity, anisotropy). — [ISTA](https://research-explorer.ista.ac.at/record/11736)
- Wang 2011 states its model ignores cloth memory/plasticity; only static parameters measured. — [PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7g35q2x7/qt7g35q2x7.pdf)
- Gong 2026 models friction-based internal dissipation and yield/hardening from cyclic tests; shows cotton and linen wrinkle most, wool and silk resist; PET fine creases only. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13707)
- Clyde 2017 uses polynomial-like nonlinear exponent models per mode (warp, weft, area, shear), i.e. a J-curve-capable hyperelastic form for wovens. — [paper](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/CTT17.pdf)

### Inferences
- [inf] Our optional J-curve is consistent with the literature; calibrate it against digitised curves from Sperl 2022 / Wang piecewise-linear tables rather than guesswork.
- [inf] Hysteresis can be postponed; for rest-state wrinkle persistence use friction/damping as a cheap proxy and track Gong's dataset release.

### Gaps
- No quantitative KES hysteresis (2HB, 2HG) to simulator-parameter mapping located; Uçar 2003 gives knit KES values only (see section 4).

## 3. Bending models and mesh dependence

### Takeaway
Dihedral hinge models are cheap but mesh-structure-sensitive; quadratic/cubic shells are valid only for isometric deformation and over-stiffen otherwise (about 3x energy overestimate on linear plate tests per one 2025 study); wrinkles are not resolved on coarse meshes: in a recent benchmark wrinkling first appears around 40x40 nodes, converges beyond 80x80, and below 20x20 shows heavy artifacts.

### Cited Findings
- **Corotational hinge-based thin plates/shells** (authors not given on fetched page; HKU & TransGP; ~2025; arXiv 2502.10872). Six models (edge, FVM, smoothed hinge). Edge-based hinge models are sensitive to mesh structure; triangle-centred ones less so; **Quadratic and Cubic Shell models overestimate bending energy by ~3x and fail all linear plate tests**; cantilever tip deflection vs ABAQUS S4R reference 6.012: EP 5.388, ES 5.387, FP 6.056, FS 6.072, SP 6.055, SS 6.072, Quadratic/Cubic Shell 2.510. Twisted-cylinder wrinkle count 19 (ES, FS) vs 20 (SS). Code: https://github.com/lianggx-hku/libThinPlateShells (licence not seen). Effort: S-M to port a constant-Hessian hinge model [inf]. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10872v7)
- **Meng, Shi, Huang, Lu, Guo, Komura, Yang, Li, "A B-spline finite element method for cloth simulation", arXiv 2506.18867 (2025).** Quadratic B-spline (C1) elements with Bergou-style quadratic bending; linear FEM on alternating-diagonal meshes fails to converge on plate bending; wrinkling sheet: <20x20 significant artifacts, wrinkling first at 40x40, converged beyond 80x80; up to 2x faster for visual parity; code promised, not linked. Effort: L-XL (new discretisation). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18867v3)
- **Grinspun, Hirani, Desbrun, Schroder 2003, "Discrete shells", SCA.** Hinge (dihedral) energy [bg; not fetched]. **Bergou, Wardetzky, Harmon, Zorin, Grinspun 2006, "A quadratic bending model for inextensible surfaces", SGP** — energy quadratic in positions, constant Hessian, isometric assumption. — [Zorin page](https://cims.nyu.edu/~dzorin/e/bergou2006qbm/index.html), [EG DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/edfc6fd8-504e-4c0c-89d6-35579d1c8b39). **Garg, Grinspun, Wardetzky, Zorin 2007, "Cubic shells", SCA** — [EG DL](https://diglib.eg.org/items/edae36de-d220-49b1-9939-fb292202ee90/full), [PDF](https://cs.nyu.edu/~dzorin/papers/garg2007cs.pdf). (I only saw listings of these; details not read. Bridson 2003 not located.)
- Larionov 2022 uses a dihedral-angle bending constraint with scalar stiffness, recovered within 4 % on synthetic data. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08790)
- Ju 2024 industry-style simulator uses piecewise-constant two-piece bending (15 deg threshold). — [paper](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.1111/cgf15027/v43i2_20_15027.pdf)
- Clyde 2017 flags Kirchhoff-Love bending assumptions as a limit for small-strain bend test fits. — [paper](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/CTT17.pdf)
- CIPC (codimensional IPC) surfaced in the search for mesh dependence but was not read. — [arXiv 2012.04457](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04457)

### Inferences
- [inf] Key product risk: a fabric with B ~1e-5 N*m has buckle wavelength of order mm to a few cm; typical garment meshes (5-10 mm edges) under-resolve it, so measured-bending parameters will produce stiffer-looking, fewer-wrinkle results. Mitigation ideas (unverified, none sourced as "known scaling fix" in this session): (a) validate cantilever overhang convergence under refinement and fit a per-mesh-size bending correction; (b) mesh-adaptive refinement near compressed regions; (c) switch to smoother elements. I found no source for a published universal scaling fix; mark as open.
- [inf] Cantilever overhang test is a nearly mesh-independent check for the bending model (a smooth, low-curvature solution); Cusick/wrinkle tests are the mesh-sensitive ones. Run both across 2-3 resolutions.
- [inf] If the current bending is an edge hinge on unstructured meshes, test an alternating-diagonal vs equilateral mesh; the 2025 study suggests different results.

- No source found for anisotropic bending in discrete shells specifically (Wang 2011 and Gong 2026 show anisotropy is needed; hinge-energy anisotropy implementation not located).
- Wrinkle-wavelength vs element-size scaling law (e.g. lambda ~ (B/K)^(1/4)-type) not sourced here; do not use without a citation.
- Bridson 2003 ("Simulation of clothing with folds and wrinkles") not located.

## 4. In-plane models and validation reference values (Cusick, cantilever, KES)

### Takeaway
There is enough published data to build regression tests with tolerance bands, but few papers give per-fabric simulated-vs-measured numbers; the best numbers: Kenkare 2008 measured DC ranges for 14 fabrics, Kim & Lee 2024 give CLO-virtual vs real DC (0.674 vs 0.762 mean, r=0.824, error grows with stiffness), Buyukaslan 2018 shows node count matches but drape area does not.

### Cited Findings
- **Stiff-vs-compressed in-plane:** Clyde models compression/Poisson as unmeasured (a12 assumed); CLO documents a separate "buckling stiffness" parameter (title only seen). - **Measured Cusick DC (%) by fabric (Kenkare et al. 2008, J. Textile Inst. 99(3):211-218; 36 cm sample, 18 cm disk, 12 trials each; Cusick vs 3D scan correlation 0.9842):** Plain 1: 27-36 (avg 33); Interlock 6: 27-38 (35); Rib 211: 15-21 (19); Lawn: 43-54 (50); Plain 2: 46-63 (52); Challis: 26-34 (29); Twill 1: 42-51 (49); Plain 4: 46-63 (59); Oxford 5: 42-63 (58); Polytwill: 35-40 (36); Corduroy: 67-75 (71); Momie: 77-83 (79); Twill 3: 70-83 (76); Plain 20: 73-85 (79). ### Proposed automated regression suite (all [inf] design, anchored to above)
1. **Cantilever (fast, near mesh-independent):** simulate D1388 strip, compare overhang/bending length to analytic Peirce from the preset's B (tolerance +/-5 %; Clyde shows fitted bend angles can miss by tens of percent, so test the implementation, not the fit); run at 3 mesh sizes.
2. **Cusick drape:** 30 or 36 cm disk on 18 cm support; output DC (area ratio), node count, fold amplitude; compare to preset-specific bands (e.g. limp chiffon-like: DC low-to-mid; stiff canvas-like DC ~0.7-0.85 by Kenkare's stiff plain/twill 70-85 %) with +/-10 DC points tolerance given measured spread; separately track "mesh-refinement convergence" of node count.
3. **Tensile/shear curve fits:** reproduce preset stress-strain at warp/weft/bias within Clyde-level 2-5 %.
4. **Wrinkling sheet resolution study** (Meng 2025: wrinkles appear >=40x40, converge >=80x80) as a mesh-dependence canary.
5. **Rest/settling:** time-to-rest and residual kinetic energy for a draped sheet; pendulum/hanging-sheet swing decay (damping ratio vs coefficient). No reference values found for real fabric damping ratios.
6. **Monotonicity:** DC increases as B decreases; correlation to B at least r~0.8 (Kim & Lee: real-virtual r=0.824).

### Inferences
- [inf] Because CLO-class simulators over-drape (lower DC) for stiff fabrics, our solver should be validated on stiff presets (canvas, denim, leather) first; those are the failure region in the literature.
- [inf] Node count is a more robust target than DC (Buyukaslan: nodes matched while area did not).
- [inf] Preset sanity: at 3000 N/m tensile, 1e-5 N*m bending, the ratio is atypical for stiff fabrics; check each of the ten presets against Kenkare-range B (about 3.5e-6 to 5.8e-5 N*m if units as I read them).

### Gaps
- No per-fabric published DC for chiffon, poplin, denim, silk charmeuse, wool suiting, leather found with sources (only Kenkare's anonymised fabrics and Kim & Lee silk grades). No Cusick-validation papers by names "Bhatia", "Mei", "Liu", "Sidorov" were located (Bhatia/Mei/Liu searches returned none); Lee et al. not located.
- ASTM D1388/BS 3356 numeric reference values per fabric not found (the fibre2fashion article lists none).

## 5. Rest and damping (failure 1)

### Takeaway
Source coverage is thin. The one rigorous located source argues that Rayleigh damping couples all deformation modes and recommends per-mode dissipation potentials (stretch, bending, shear separately); air drag is a separate force and not addressed there.

### Cited Findings
- Sanchez-Banderas & Otaduy 2017 (CEIG, Eurographics proceedings): per-mode dissipation potentials proportional to strain rate; Rayleigh damping raises the damping ratio in both calibration and test scenes while their model leaves the test scene ~constant; small-angle (<5 deg) vector strain form avoids undefined gradient at zero; test: 1 ms step, density 130 kg/m^3, backward Euler with one Newton step; air drag not modelled (only Terzopoulos-style viscous drag mentioned); plan to estimate damping from measurements is future work. — [PDF](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/ceig20171202/011-018.pdf)
- Wang 2011 measures only static parameters; damping is out of scope. — [PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7g35q2x7/qt7g35q2x7.pdf)
- An NVIDIA forum thread distinguishes "drag" vs "damping" in PBD material parameters (opened only as a search hit, not read). — [forum](https://forums.developer.nvidia.com/t/understanding-the-difference-between-drag-and-damping-in-pbd-material-parameters/311117)
- Gong 2026: hysteresis-like internal friction yields persistent wrinkles; time/temperature/moisture effects not modelled. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13707)

### Inferences
- [inf] Use per-mode (stretch/shear/bend) viscous damping rather than mass-proportional Rayleigh; add small air drag (relative velocity, area-weighted) for settling of light fabrics; measure with a pendulum/swing decay test. Static friction handles the final stick. Real damping values must be set from experiments we do not have.

### Gaps
- No sourced real-fabric damping ratios, aerodynamic drag coefficients, or Style3D/CLO damping definitions located.
