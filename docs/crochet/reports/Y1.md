# Y1 — The rod core (docs/YARN_MODE_PLAN.md sections 3 and 7)

A Cosserat rod solver in C++ for yarn mode: `crochet/rod.hpp`, `crochet_rod.cpp`, `test_crochet_rod.cpp`. Nothing of it is wired into the editor yet.

## What CrochetPARADE gave us (read before writing Y1)

`third_party/crochetparade/` (GPL-3.0-or-later, into our AGPL-3.0 work; recorded in `THIRD_PARTY.md` section 8). Its engine has **no yarn model**: a
stitch is a pair of nodes (the V at the top and the attachment below) and a length, and the layout is distance geometry (an annealed all-pairs MDS with
a log-barrier repulsion, then a damped spring relaxation). So there is no rod, twist or strand contact to take, and **no CrochetPARADE code is in Y1**.
What is useful, and where it goes:

* **Stitch heights as ratios** (`parse64.js`, the built-in stitch table): sc 1, hdc 1.5, dc 2, sl st 0.4, post stitches offset 0.3 / 0.4 / 0.6 toward
  the front or back. Y2 and Y4 take these as the first guess for the height of the loop a macro pulls up, to be judged by eye at the Y2 gate.
* **The stitch grammar's vocabulary**: a stitch has a top node (the V, where the hook goes in) and a bottom node that attaches to the top node of an
  earlier stitch, with an "attachment depth" for post stitches. That is the same idea as our `insert(opening)`: the pickable opening is the top node's
  loop. The Y3 picking and the text round trip should keep to it so a clicked pattern stays a CrochetPARADE pattern.
* **Inflate, then settle** (`docs/node_layout_documentation_v2.md`, section 4.3): switch the non-neighbour repulsion on strongly at first so the shape
  opens up into a coherent whole, then let it fall away. It is the same idea as the plan's slack and thickness sliders; for yarn it is a way to untangle
  a bad start. Not needed for Y1 (hard contact and a non-intersecting start do the job); noted for Y2's relaxation of a freshly made swatch.
* **Damped second-order relaxation with the drag treated implicitly** (section 6.3, `v <- (dt f + 2 v) / (2 + dt gamma)`): our substeps use
  `exp(-gamma h)`, which does the same job. Nothing to change.
* **Front/back loop displacement by a plane-normal offset** (section 7): in a rod model the displaced bar of a BLO or FLO stitch comes out of the
  geometry, so it becomes a check for Y4 (the bar sits in front of or behind the plane of the work) and not a constraint.

## What was built

`RodSystem`: particles joined by segments, one orientation quaternion per segment (its local z is the tangent), chains open or closed, any number of
them in one system.

* **Stretch-shear** per segment, **bend-twist** per joint (Kugelstadt and Schomer 2016), on XPBD (Macklin et al. 2016): compliances come straight from
  `EA`, `GAs`, `EI`, `GJ` (energy 1/2 EA l eps^2, 1/2 (EI/l) phi^2, 1/2 (GJ/l) phi^2), so a stiffness means what it says.
* **A direct solve per open chain** (Deul et al. 2018): the constraints ordered s0, j0, s1, j1, ... make `J W J^T + alpha` block pentadiagonal with
  3 x 3 blocks, solved by a block LDL^T in O(n). This was not in the first draft. The first version swept the constraints one by one (Gauss-Seidel) and
  failed the physics: a 60-segment chain hung over a span stretched 2.5 % and sagged 7 % too much, and a clamped rod hung limp (tip at 10.3 cm against
  0.47 cm by beam theory), because a sweep carries stretch and bend along a chain one segment at a time. Closed chains and `direct = false` still sweep.
* **Capsule contact** between segments of any chains (a spatial hash for the pairs, closest points of two segments), with Coulomb friction on the
  displacement of the contact points; segments of one chain within `contactSkipSegments` of each other do not touch. A move cap per substep
  (0.4 radius) against tunnelling. A floor plane for particles.
* **Twist**: `cumulativeTwist` and `jointRotation` read the twist back. The yarn's own ply twist is deliberately not a rod twist (a ply turn per 2 mm
  segment is more than a quaternion holds across a joint); the rod carries the twist added or removed, the renderer adds the plies.
* Helpers: `linkingNumber` (Gauss integral of two closed polygons), `minStrandGap`, `closestSegmentSegment`.

## Limits, stated

* The bend-twist Jacobian is the small-angle one; a joint turned by more than about a half turn is outside the quaternion's range (the clamped-twist test
  uses 2.8 rad). A yarn does not bend that sharply per 2 mm.
* Timing is for the swatch size the plan names; 79 ms leaves little room for the renderer and the hook. Substeps and iterations can be cut (the direct
  solve is stable with few), and a thread-per-chain or a coarser contact pass are the next knobs (Y6).
* The yarn defaults (EI, GJ, EA, density, friction) are starter values, not measured on a real strand.
* Closed chains use the sweep, so a closed ring is softer than an open chain of the same stiffness. Yarn mode works with one open yarn; rings occur only in the tests.
