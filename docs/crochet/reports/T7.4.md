# T7.4 — Surface shader and double-sided thickness (§8.3)

The crochet view now draws the cloth as two shells (RS and WS) offset along the vertex normal by half the yarn thickness, instead of
one flat sheet, so the cut edge reads as a thick fabric and the two faces can be coloured separately.

* **Shader** `godot/crochet/crochet_surface.gdshader`: per stitch cell, procedural relief. On the RS a "V" of two tilted ellipses on
  a post with a fine diagonal yarn twist; on the WS horizontal bars and the post; a BLO row adds a raised ridge on the RS, a FLO row
  one on the WS. Uniforms `albedo`, `side` (0 RS shell, 1 WS shell), `rough`, `sheen` (rim light), `relief` (0 turns it off), `twist`;
  `_apply_yarn_look()` sets them from the chosen yarn. The shells are built in `_update_mesh` (`crochet_main.gd`): RS outside, offset
  along the normal by half the thickness; WS inside, offset against it, winding reversed.
* **Per-vertex data from the extension**: `CrochetScene.get_vertex_uv()` (stitch coordinates, one unit per stitch),
  `get_vertex_colors()` (R = stitch type: 0.2 ch, 0.4 sl st, 0.6 sc, 0.7 hdc, 0.8 dc, 1.0 tr; G = insertion: 0 both loops, 0.33 BLO,
  0.66 FLO, 1 space; B = 1 on a RS row), `get_thickness()` (m, the largest material thickness).
* **Stitch UV rule** (`fl::stitchUv`, `crochet_layout.cpp`): Flat layouts use (−X/w, −Y/h) so the UV follows the stitch grid on the
  flat piece; revolve layouts use (stitch index in the round, row). UV is per stitch, so it does not change while the cloth moves.
* **Camera**: the framing is refit when a run finishes (`_finish_run`), because the hung pouch is much longer than the relaxed one.
