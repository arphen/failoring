# Converge the solver, smooth the contact

**The literature points to one dominant fix and one dominant gap.** Failure 1 (never at rest) is most plausibly a property of the current method: one linearised Newton solve per substep, a 100-iteration capped block-Jacobi CG and a 3 % strain-limit projection leave a residual that is re-injected every substep (this diagnosis is the researchers' inference; no paper tests it on this stack). The principled remedies are cheap and sit inside the current architecture: carry the unresolved residual forward or converge the final substep ([Lan et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19272v4)), use an energy-decreasing step, use per-mode rather than Rayleigh damping ([Sanchez-Banderas & Otaduy 2017](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/ceig20171202/011-018.pdf)), and replace on/off contact with smooth barrier or offset contact so the armpit pinch stops flickering between active sets. The best cost-for-robustness contact method found is Offset Geometric Contact (OGC, SIGGRAPH 2025), which runs a cloth-on-body scene in **9.6 ms per frame on an RTX 4090 versus 24 s for IPC** ([OGC paper](https://graphics.utah.edu/research/projects/ogc/Offset_Geometric_Contact-SIGGRAPH2025.pdf)), but no code is released. For resolution (failure 2) there is no published way to get 1.5 cm gathers in real time on a laptop; the evidence supports coarse-to-fine settling plus local refinement in gather zones instead. For materials (failure 5) the existing parameter family is sufficient for limp-to-medium fabrics, and the weakest link is bending, so the first investment is an automated cantilever and Cusick regression suite. For engine (F), the notes support upgrading Godot to a release with the native Metal driver and re-measuring before any rewrite. **The gap that governs every Apple judgement in this report: no source located reports cloth, IPC, VBD or any listed solver running on an Apple GPU, Metal or MoltenVK.** All headline speeds are CUDA on RTX 3090/4090-class hardware, so they are upper bounds for an M3. All effort sizes (S, M, L, XL) and all Metal-suitability judgements below are the researchers' inferences, not sourced.

## Why a capped single linear solve cannot settle, and what rests reliably

**Plain VBD is the wrong default for near-inextensible woven cloth.** Its authors report poor convergence at a 1:10,000 stiffness ratio, excessive extension at 100 iterations, no guarantee for infinitely stiff constraints, and information that travels only about one colour-count per iteration ([VBD, arXiv 2403.06321](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.06321)). Its one cloth scene is small (2,500 vertices, 20 iterations, 11.2 ms per step; the paper lists it as a CPU row), and its large GPU numbers are volumetric (15K-vertex Armadillo 3.3 ms per step on an RTX 4090). Without damping, kinetic energy persists a long time, and per-vertex Rayleigh damping speeds settling. Failure 1's 14 mm tiered-dress swing is consistent with under-converged stiff modes, which is the researchers' reading rather than a measured result.

The follow-ups attack exactly the stiff regime but are CUDA-first. Augmented VBD adds an augmented Lagrangian for hard constraints, demonstrated on rigid blocks (110,000 blocks at 3.5 ms per frame on a 4090, stable stacking at 4 iterations); the page read does not show cloth, and the demo repositories state no licence ([AVBD page](https://graphics.cs.utah.edu/research/projects/avbd/)). JGS2 from Style3D gets Newton-like convergence through a rest-shape subspace: stiff Armadillo in 64 iterations against Newton's 58 while VBD fails to converge in 10,000, and a 2M-triangle tablecloth with IPC contact at **469 ms per frame on an RTX 3090**, but with a 48-minute CPU precompute that conflicts with live pattern editing ([JGS2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06494v1)). Lan et al. reach the same end through projective dynamics with subspace reuse and residual forwarding: a 40K-vertex drape at 25 FPS and a 340K-vertex fashion show at 4.8 FPS on an RTX 3090, about 20 iterations against more than 1,000 without the subspace ([arXiv 2403.19272](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.19272v4)). Residual forwarding is the item most directly relevant to failure 1, because it removes the systematic error left by early termination. Both the subspace methods require a quadratic elastic model, so the current anisotropic FEM with compression asymmetry and a J-curve would need a projective-dynamics-compatible form (researchers' inference). A 2026 Periodic Anderson Acceleration paper cuts energy 6-49 % against VBD with Chebyshev on tetrahedral meshes, but both variants diverged in a contact-heavy test, which warns against stacking acceleration on contact-active vertices ([PAA](https://graphics.cs.uh.edu/wp-content/papers/2026/2026-HPG-PAA-PhysicalSim.pdf)).

For inextensibility without a post-hoc projection, the classic route is constraint-based: Goldenthal et al. solve inextensible cloth with a fast projection inside implicit integration ([project page](https://www.cs.columbia.edu/cg/ESIC/esic.html)) with a parallel follow-up ([VRIPHYS 2008](https://animation.rwth-aachen.de/media/papers/2008-VRIPHYS-Parallel_Inextensible_Cloth.pdf)). The notes could not retrieve either algorithm, so the mechanics are unverified. XPBD and Small Steps (Macklin et al., SCA 2019, [DOI listing](https://diglib.eg.org/handle/10.1145/3309486-3340247)) share the many-substeps structure of the current fast solve, but the paper content was not retrieved. The VBD paper reports XPBD 10x slower per frame than VBD in a squishy-ball test (0.32 s versus 0.031 s) and crushing a 1:2000 mass-ratio cube that VBD handled.

Contact smoothness is the second half of the rest problem. The pinch diagnosis (cloth squeezed between arm and torso SDF regions, with penalty or Coulomb contact alternating between active sets) is the researchers' inference and untested. The supporting mechanism is that IPC's smooth barrier and OGC's two-stage C2 activation remove the on/off switch ([OGC paper](https://graphics.utah.edu/research/projects/ogc/Offset_Geometric_Contact-SIGGRAPH2025.pdf)). Lagged friction can still micro-slip at rest unless iterated to convergence, so a sleep rule remains advisable (unsourced). No source reports a quantitative resting-jitter metric.

## Smooth contact is affordable only as OGC-style bounds, not as full IPC

**Full IPC-class contact is an offline or settle-to-rest tool here.** Every GPU IPC implementation found is CUDA-only and measured on a 4090. GIPC averages 46.7x over its CPU reference ([arXiv 2308.09400](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.09400), code [GPU_IPC](https://github.com/KemengHuang/GPU_IPC), MPL-2.0). StiffGIPC claims up to 10x over GIPC with a C2 strain-limiting energy for stiff cloth, but a 265K-triangle scene costs 1.56 s per 5 ms step ([arXiv 2411.06224](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.06224)). Barrier-Augmented Lagrangian is up to 3.8x faster than GIPC with no cloth results ([arXiv 2407.00046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.00046)), and AGIPC (SIGGRAPH 2026) adds up to about 3x over StiffGIPC ([arXiv 2605.04773](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.04773)). The best direct data point in the 10-50k vertex band is YASPS: a cloth-on-bunny at 39.6k vertices costs about 0.7 s per frame and at 60k about 1.6 s on a 4090 ([arXiv 2605.23088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23088)). An M3 is likely 10x or more slower (researchers' inference). Codim-IPC contributes what a garment app needs conceptually: explicit thickness consistent from 1 to 10 mm stacks, a C2 strain-limiting barrier and ACCD ([arXiv 2012.04457](https://arxiv.org/abs/2012.04457), code [Codim-IPC](https://github.com/ipc-sim/Codim-IPC), Apache-2.0, only 5 commits visible).

OGC is the standout. Each face is offset along its normal, a C2 activation gives the normal energy, and per-vertex displacement bounds truncate solver steps so CCD is avoided. The paper reports cloth on a body at **9.6 ms per frame on a 4090 (Warp)** and 0.24 s on a Ryzen 7950X CPU against 24 s for IPC, a 13.8K-vertex robot and T-shirt scene at 1.8 ms per step, and 50 cloth layers (246K vertices) at 6.3 ms per step. Friction is IPC-style lagged friction tested at mu 0.1 to 0.5. Two limits matter directly to this product: with a prescribed-motion body, penetration of the body can still occur, and guarantees assume an intersection-free start. The paper says C++ and Warp versions are planned, but the project page lists no code or licence ([project page](https://graphics.utah.edu/research/projects/ogc/)). The practical path is therefore to link [ipc-toolkit](https://pypi.org/project/ipctk) (MIT, v1.6.1 released 22 Oct 2026, macOS ARM64 wheels; barrier, ACCD and friction potentials, no solver) into the existing CPU Newton solve now, then reimplement OGC's displacement bounds from the paper (effort M then M-L, inferred).

Cheaper fallbacks and classics fill specific gaps. Li et al. 2018 gives exact Coulomb friction for cloth-body and cloth-cloth contact, the best-fit classic for sliding on skin (failure 3), but code and licence were not found and the rewrite is large ([SIGGRAPH history](https://history.siggraph.org/?p=100711)). Bridson, Fedkiw and Anderson 2002 supplies thickness-aware robust collision treatment ([PDF](https://www.cs.ubc.ca/~rbridson/docs/cloth2002.pdf); mechanisms not read). Intersection Contour Minimization untangles history-free ([Ye & Zhao 2012](https://diglib.eg.org/items/7cf03072-dbcd-4705-8f12-b77760e392ae)), which suits a last-resort recovery pass. A fast GPU two-way CCD scheme reaches 0.034 s per step on a 71K-vertex bow knot on an RTX 2080 Ti, with grid-hash proximity search, no code ([arXiv 2211.04045](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2211.04045)). No source gives a dedicated armpit or crotch method, no source benchmarks per-frame SDF baking for a skinned body, and none covers friction against moving skin; the researchers' suggestions (sub-step the body pose, clamp per-vertex displacement, sample body velocity for relative sliding, keep the cloth offset larger than the narrowest arm-torso gap) are unsourced engineering.

## Gathers, ruffles and sewing: edge-aligned meshes and rest-length seams beat brute resolution

**No public method gets 1.5 cm gathers in real time; the published tricks change the mesh instead.** CLO's shirring patent US11461967B2 generates inner lines perpendicular to the shirring area, realigns mesh polygons so one side lies on each line, and notes that smaller mesh in the area helps pleat detail ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US11461967)). That is a rest-state trick, and a cheap analogue is edge-aligned fine triangles in gather zones. CLO's pre-positioning is template-driven: a pattern-block database where edges carry sewing information and blocks carry position information for arrangement around the avatar ([US12371830B2](https://patents.google.com/patent/US12371830B2/en)), built on the Volino and Magnenat-Thalmann pipeline (arrange, collide, remove collisions by force, drape). The patent states that prior methods needed manual seam selection and arrangement. Style3D documents "Auto Arrange (Beta)": freeze a reference piece, then propagate through sewing relationships ([help page](https://help.style3d.com/studio/en/c2c8/5869/0792/ebff/13f8/12d5)), a seam-graph traversal that is easy to copy. Seam strength ramps, spring-versus-constraint formulation and thickness handling are not published for CLO, Marvelous Designer or Style3D; Style3D's blog claims seam types, stitch density and allowance but gives no mechanism and should be treated as marketing ([blog](https://www.style3d.com/blog/?p=13118)).

GarmentCode is the best open reference for programmatic placement and stitching: panels carry a 6D placement relative to an SMPL body, vertices are added so stitched edges have equal counts, and gathers are stitches between unequal-length interfaces ([arXiv 2306.03642](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.03642v2); code [GarmentCode](https://github.com/maria-korosteleva/GarmentCode), MIT per the repo sidebar). Its stated limits include no multi-layer stitching, no fold lines, and limited pleats and smocking. Its dataset drapes come from a Warp XPBD simulator at about 30 s per garment on an RTX 3090 with fixed stiffness and three bending softnesses ([GarmentCodeData, arXiv 2405.17609](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.17609)), so it is a weak teacher for a fabric-calibrated FEM product. For layered outfits the one relevant data point is ContourCraft's failure case: it fails if layer order cannot be inferred from the initial geometry, which supports explicit layer ordering at placement ([arXiv 2405.09522](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09522v2)). Quasi-static settling, damping ramps and pre-folded starts were not found in any located source; coarse-to-fine settling, a heavy-damping initial phase and a shrinking seam rest-length are the researchers' engineering suggestions.

Adaptive remeshing is the classical answer to failure 2. ARCSim offers anisotropic remeshing aligned with wrinkles, physics-aware projection for stiff materials and self-CCD, but its licence is free for non-profit use only, so it is study material rather than code to ship ([ARCSim](https://graphics.berkeley.edu/resources/ARCSim/index.html)). A full interactive reimplementation is XL; a reduced version (isotropic refinement at seams and high curvature during the initial drape only) is M (inferred), and remeshing mid-simulation breaks persistent seam and contact topology and forces re-analysis of the supernodal factorisation. For detail without resolution, three tiers exist: geometric wrinkling driven by the coarse sim's stretch tensor ([Rohmer et al. 2010](https://imagine.inrialpes.fr/people/Damien.Rohmer/documents/publications/10_sigasia_wrinkle/10_sigasia_wrinkle.html)), learned linear upsampling ([Kavan et al. 2011](https://users.cs.utah.edu/~ladislav/kavan11physics/kavan11physics.html), 0.8 ms single-thread, **patented as US8831919B2** per [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US8831919), a freedom-to-operate concern), and neural super-resolution ([GDSR, SIGGRAPH Asia 2024](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.06285v1)) at 5,010 to 43,257 vertices in 0.115 s per frame total on an RTX 3090, of which 0.083 s is the Marvelous Designer low-resolution sim. GDSR is trained on a single fabric, cannot reproduce friction wrinkles and leaves multi-layer collisions unresolved. No located source addresses gather or ruffle-specific wrinkles.

## Neural previews are not ready for arbitrary user patterns

HOOD is the only self-supervised network whose inputs (rest edge lengths, per-edge materials) suit arbitrary patterns: 7.27 fps on a roughly 12K-vertex dress, trained on SMPL bodies and AMASS motion, with no garment-garment interaction ([HOOD summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2212.07242.md); code [HOOD](https://github.com/Dolorousrtur/HOOD), MIT label, but the paper says weights are for research purposes, so verify before commercial use). ContourCraft handles multi-layer outfits at 10.5 fps (12,030 nodes) to 3.7 fps (36,515 nodes, five garments) on an RTX 4090, cuts intersecting pairs from about 8,796 to 150, and was rated 4.15 against CLO3D's 4.29 in a perceptual study, with participants preferring CLO3D head to head ([arXiv 2405.09522](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.09522v2)). HyperBones reaches about 1.1 ms on an A6000 for body-skinned garments but has no self-collision and no automatic new garments ([summary](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.20460.md)). None is tested on user-edited flat patterns with gathers, none targets static equilibrium, and no Apple MPS, MLX or CoreML timing exists for any cloth network. The more promising hybrid in the researchers' view is a learned warm start trained on the team's own converged drapes (inference; no published recipe). At the GarmentCodeData cost anchor, 50K drapes at 30 s each is about 420 GPU-hours (arithmetic from the cited figure, not a prediction for this solver). Training on SMPL bodies would need retraining for a MakeHuman-derived body, and SMPL, LLaVA and CAESAR-derived licences are unverified blockers. LLM pattern generators such as AIpparel (2.1 s per garment on an A6000, about 250 tokens per pattern, [arXiv 2412.03937](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.03937)) matter for the agent-editing story, not for solver speed.

## Materials: bending is the weak link, and presets may be mis-scaled

**The parameter family is the right one; bending calibration and mesh dependence are the risks.** Fitted tensile curves reproduce measurements within a few percent (Clyde et al. 2017: 0.8-5.1 % mean relative error across five fabrics, off-axis 5.6-12.8 % against experimental scatter of 11-42 %) but their simulated bend angle often missed the 41.5 degree target, for example 54.6 degrees warp versus 23.7 weft on canvas ([paper](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/CTT17.pdf)). Wang, O'Brien and Ramamoorthi 2011 show bending varies with bias angle, a feature absent from a warp/weft-only model ([PDF](https://escholarship.org/content/qt7g35q2x7/qt7g35q2x7.pdf)). Larionov et al. fit wrinkle-robust parameters by comparing FFT magnitudes of vertex grids, reaching 3.7 mm maximum vertex error on real swatches ([arXiv 2212.08790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.08790)), a reusable loss idea. Industry practice confirms the pattern: CLO's own team tunes bending against Cusick silhouettes, predicting bending from drape at R-squared 0.937 while stretch only reaches 0.58-0.65 ([Ju et al. 2024](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.1111/cgf15027/v43i2_20_15027.pdf)).

CLO-class simulators over-drape stiff fabrics: mean virtual drape coefficient 0.6735 against 0.7619 real across 30 silks, r = 0.824, agreement best for the most limp grades ([Kim & Lee 2024](https://fashionandtextiles.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40691-024-00388-6)), and node count matches real fabric even when drape area does not ([Buyukaslan et al. 2018](https://research.itu.edu.tr/en/publications/comparative-analysis-of-drape-characteristics-of-actually-and-vir/)). The researchers' unit conversion shows the current **1e-5 N·m bending is about 0.10 gf·cm²/cm, a chiffon-class value**, giving a bending length near 22 mm at 100 g/m² (their arithmetic, unverified); Kenkare's measured fabrics span roughly 3.5e-6 to 5.8e-5 N·m if the printed dyne-cm units are per cm width (also unverified) ([Kenkare et al. 2008](https://mae.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2016/08/JTIPart12008.pdf)). So each of the ten presets, and especially a single shared bending of 1e-5, deserves an audit.

Mesh dependence is documented and bites at this product's resolutions. In a 2025 hinge-model study, quadratic and cubic shells overestimated bending energy about 3x and failed all linear plate tests, while triangle-centred hinge models were less mesh-sensitive than edge-based ones (cantilever tip deflection 6.07 against 6.012 reference for the best models versus 2.51 for quadratic/cubic shells; [arXiv 2502.10872](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.10872v7), code [libThinPlateShells](https://github.com/lianggx-hku/libThinPlateShells), licence not seen). A B-spline FEM study finds wrinkling first appears at about 40x40 nodes, converges beyond 80x80 and shows heavy artefacts below 20x20 ([arXiv 2506.18867](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18867v3)). This is a strong argument that part of failure 2 is a bending-discretisation artefact, not just insufficient resolution. No published universal scaling fix was found, and a wavelength-versus-element-size law was not sourced, so none should be assumed. Hysteresis and persistent wrinkles are only now being modelled (Gong et al. 2026, a preprint with a dataset promised on acceptance; [arXiv 2609.13707](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.13707)), and can wait. For rest behaviour, per-mode dissipation potentials outperformed Rayleigh damping in calibration and test scenes ([Sanchez-Banderas & Otaduy](https://diglib7.eg.org/bitstream/handle/10.2312/ceig20171202/011-018.pdf)); no real-fabric damping ratios or air-drag coefficients were found.

## Rendering: sheen and weave maps live, a path tracer for stills

**CLO-level stills come mostly from the lighting and the renderer, not from novel fabric research.** CLO integrates Chaos V-Ray through its AppSDK ([Chaos blog](https://www.chaos.com/blog/how-clo-has-integrated-v-ray-in-the-fabric-of-its-software)); Style3D says previews are rasterised and finals ray traced but names no engine ([blog](https://www.style3d.com/blog/?p=6228)). For the live Godot view the cheapest high-value items are a Charlie sheen lobe following Estevez and Kulla ([course notes](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2017-shading-course/imageworks/s2017_pbs_imageworks_sheen.pdf)) and Filament's cloth model ([docs](https://google.github.io/filament/Filament.md.html)), plus baked procedural weave maps with an anisotropic highlight along the yarn tangent (Godot exposes ANISOTROPY and ANISOTROPY_FLOW fields), using Sadeghi's microcylinder model (about 100 ms per frame at 1 spp on a 2013 mobile GPU; distant viewing only, [PDF](https://datarepo.eng.ucsd.edu/visiondata/obisker/public_html/cloth/microcylinder-sg13.pdf)) or Irawan and Marschner ([author page](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~srm/publications/TOG12-cloth.html)). These ranks and efforts (S-M) are inferences, and Godot 4.3's lack of a built-in sheen output was not confirmed against 4.7. Godot's transparency limits are a hard constraint for chiffon: transparent materials cannot cast shadows and are excluded from SSR and refraction ([Godot docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/es/4.3/_sources/tutorials/shaders/shader_reference/spatial_shader.rst.txt)), so sheer fabrics should be treated as offline-quality. Yarn- and fibre-level models (Montazeri ply-based, [author page](https://cseweb.ucsd.edu/~henrik/papers/practical_ply_based_cloth_appearance/); Khattar multi-scale, [arXiv 2401.12724](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.12724), 3-5x faster near-field; Wu and Yuksel real-time fibre rendering, [page](https://people.csail.mit.edu/kuiwu/rtfr.html)) have no located public code, making them XL reimplementations. Neural appearance models need about 4-5 h of per-material training on an RTX 4090 ([Zeltner et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2305.02678)) and suit neither user-editable fabrics nor Apple hardware.

For stills on Apple Silicon, Cycles with MetalRT as default on M3 and newer is the mature option ([Blender PR 114296](https://projects.blender.org/blender/blender/pulls/114296)), with Open Image Denoise having Metal support ([Phoronix](https://www.phoronix.com/news/Intel-OIDn-Metal-More-ARM64)). Mitsuba 3.9.0 (26 Jun 2026) ships Metal variants with hardware ray tracing ([release notes](https://mitsuba.readthedocs.io/en/v3.9.1/release_notes.html)), attractive for custom cloth BSDFs but only months old (high risk, inferred). Octane X is commercial and closed. No M3 samples-per-pixel or wall-clock numbers for garment scenes were located, and the Cycles and Mitsuba licences were not verified here. On the avatar, MakeHuman system assets and skins 01-03 are CC0 ([asset packs](https://static.makehumancommunity.org/assets/assetpacks/index.html)), and the selfie-to-avatar route located (CAP4D, [GitHub](https://github.com/felixtaubner/cap4d/)) needs FLAME tracking, CUDA PyTorch3D and hours of runtime. Skin SSS, eye shading and uncanny-valley mitigation had no located sources; a stylised, cleanly shaded body is the researchers' unsourced recommendation.

## The engine question: upgrade Godot first, keep it as the shell

Godot added a native Metal backend (Apple Silicon only) in the 4.4 cycle, claimed at least as fast as Vulkan, with Intel Macs staying on MoltenVK ([Godot 4.4 dev 1](https://godotengine.org/article/dev-snapshot-godot-4-4-dev-1/)). The release list shows 4.7.2-stable as latest ([Godot releases](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/releases)), so the project's 4.3 is about four feature releases behind and still on MoltenVK (inferred). The dispatch and readback overheads the team measured were on MoltenVK, so re-measuring on 4.7 is the cheapest experiment (inferred, not measured). One forum post reports `BufferGetDataAsync` never firing its callback on a 64 MB buffer in 4.5.1 ([forum](https://forum.godotengine.org/t/buffergetdataasync-not-executing/135711)). Whether a RenderingDevice storage buffer can back a mesh vertex buffer without copy is unverified, and no per-dispatch cost for Godot on Metal was found.

Leaving Godot is justified only if dispatch-bound after that re-measure. wgpu covers Metal, Vulkan, DX12 and browser WebGPU under Apache-2.0/MIT ([repo](https://github.com/gfx-rs/wgpu)), but a February 2026 characterisation measured WebGPU dispatch overhead at **32-71 microseconds on Metal versus 24-36 on Vulkan**, with implementation choice mattering 2.2x on Metal ([arXiv 2604.02344](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.02344)). A CG solver with hundreds of dispatches per frame therefore spends milliseconds on overhead alone, so fused kernels and GPU-side convergence decisions matter more than the engine (inferred). Direct Metal via metal-cpp, unified-memory zero-copy and indirect command buffers are plausible but no source was located. Slang targets Metal and WGSL only experimentally ([repo](https://github.com/shader-slang/slang)). NVIDIA Warp and Newton are CPU/CUDA only, with macOS on CPU ([Warp FAQ](https://nvidia.github.io/warp/stable/user_guide/faq.html); [Newton](https://github.com/newton-physics/newton), Apache-2.0), so their VBD, XPBD and Style3D projective-dynamics solvers ([solver API](https://newton-physics.github.io/newton/latest/api/newton_solvers.html)) are study material to reimplement in MSL, not a runtime. Taichi lists a Metal backend (Apache-2.0, maintenance unverified; [repo](https://github.com/taichi-dev/taichi)), and Genesis (Apache-2.0) lists Metal through its Quadrants compiler and a PBD cloth example ([repo](https://github.com/Genesis-Embodied-AI/Genesis)); neither was evaluated for a Godot-embedded product, and Python tools cannot ship into a GDExtension or WASM build without a port. Filament, bgfx, Diligent and RealityKit were not successfully researched. The cost of rewriting the editor UI in another engine was not researched either; the notes' low-confidence recommendation is that the editor is far costlier to move than the solver.

## Staged roadmap

**Stage A: quick wins in the current architecture (weeks).** Build the regression and rest suite first, because it turns every later claim into a measurement. Then make steps converge (change 2), switch seam handling to graph-propagated placement with matched vertex counts and a rest-length ramp (change 3), upgrade Godot and re-measure (change 4), audit the bending hinge and presets (change 5) and add sheen plus weave maps live (change 9). Expected unlock: a tee that rests on a standing body, cleaner gathered starts, a trustworthy fabric table. Nothing here promises 1.5 cm at real time. The coarse-to-fine settle and gather-zone refinement (change 8) belong here too and are the closest thing to a "1.5 cm look at preview cost" option, but they remain unproven (inferred).

**Stage B: a new solver core (months).** The unlocks are rest-stable drape at preview cost, layered outfits without flicker, and 10-50k-vertex outfits at interactive or near-interactive speed. Contact first: ipc-toolkit barrier and friction, then OGC-style displacement bounds with a rigid-impact-zone or ICM fallback for unplanned penetration. In parallel prototype, on CPU first, an AVBD-style stiffness ramp and Chebyshev/Anderson acceleration to learn what the stiff-cloth iteration budget really is. Only then decide whether to build a Metal GPU core, and treat the first deliverable as an M3 benchmark of the existing VBD experiment, since the notes found no Apple data at all. Neural components (a learned warm start, GDSR-style detail) are optional and sit after the solver is stable, because they need stable converged drapes as teachers.

**Stage C: the engine question (decide after A and B).** If the re-measured Godot 4.7 Metal path is dominated by dispatch or readback, move the solver into a standalone C++ core with Metal compute (native) and, if the browser matters, a wgpu/WebGPU build, keeping Godot as editor shell. If not dispatch-bound, stay. This is a low-confidence recommendation: no source gives a Godot-on-Metal dispatch figure, Metal-native dispatch cost, or the cost of rewriting the editor.

## Recommended solver architecture if rebuilding

This is a design proposal assembled from the evidence above; the pieces are individually cited but the combination has no published validation, and every Metal statement is inferred. Integrator: implicit Euler with a final-substep convergence criterion and a monotone-energy line search on the CPU reference path; on the GPU, block-Jacobi or VBD with Chebyshev, an AVBD-style stiffness ramp and residual forwarding, plus a coarse-level correction (smoothed aggregation, or a rest-shape subspace if a quadratic energy is adopted). Retain the sparse Cholesky as the exact reference and as the coarse solver. Contact: smooth C2 offset or barrier energy with per-vertex displacement bounds (OGC) and lagged IPC-style friction fed the body's surface velocity, explicit cloth thickness (Codim-IPC style) larger than the narrowest arm-torso gap, a spatial-hash broad phase, and a rigid-impact-zone or ICM fallback. Seams: constraints with rest-length scaling over many frames, seam-graph placement with a frozen reference piece and layer ordering for outfits, edge-aligned refinement in gather zones. Materials: current orthotropic family plus bias-direction bending, per-mode damping and air drag, calibrated through the simulator against cantilever and Cusick targets, bending discretisation chosen by the mesh-convergence audit. Data layout: structure-of-arrays vertex buffers in unified memory with persistent kernels, fused iterations and GPU-side convergence flags (dispatch counts matter given the measured WebGPU overheads). Editor and offline share the same solver and mesh; the offline render mode runs the same core with tighter tolerances and finer mesh, exports meshes to a path tracer (Cycles/Metal-RT with OIDN) and uses a single material description mapped to both the live shader and the path-traced BSDF, so the two renderers do not drift.

## Benchmarks to adopt

All reference values come from the notes; none comes from CLO itself, so a CLO comparison requires the team to run those scenes in CLO. Rest stability: time to rest and residual kinetic energy for a draped sheet, a standing-body tee and a tiered dress (the current 14 mm per 2 s swing is the baseline to beat), and pendulum swing decay; no published reference values exist for real-fabric damping ratios or jitter metrics. Cantilever: ASTM D1388 25x200 mm strips at the 41.5 degree plane against the analytic Peirce bending length, tolerance about 5 % to test the implementation, run at three mesh sizes ([Clyde](https://www.math.ucdavis.edu/~jteran/papers/CTT17.pdf)). Cusick drape: 30 or 36 cm specimen on an 18 cm disk, reporting drape coefficient and node count; use bands of about 10 DC points because real within-fabric spread is 10-20 points, validate stiff presets first, and use node count as the more robust target ([Kenkare](https://mae.ncsu.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/17/2016/08/JTIPart12008.pdf); [Kim & Lee](https://fashionandtextiles.springeropen.com/articles/10.1186/s40691-024-00388-6)). Tensile and bias curves within 2-5 %. Wrinkling sheet resolution study (artefacts below 20x20, onset at 40x40, converged beyond 80x80; [Meng 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.18867v3)). Monotonicity: drape coefficient must fall as bending rises, with correlation near the r = 0.824 of Kim & Lee. Performance: milliseconds per frame against vertex count at fixed substeps, comparable to published points (VBD 2.5K cloth, OGC 13.8K T-shirt at 1.8 ms, Lan 40K drape at 25 FPS, YASPS 39.6K at about 0.7 s per frame), always stating the hardware, since those are CUDA numbers. Contact: penetrating triangle pairs per frame (the ContourCraft comparison used about 8,796 versus 150) and pinch-region flicker at the armpit and crotch. Stills: noise and time per frame for a garment scene on M3, which no source provides.

## Open questions, disagreements and thin evidence

The largest unknown is Apple hardware: no Metal or MoltenVK timing exists for any cloth method, no Warp Metal roadmap was found, and the M3-versus-4090 ratio is an unverified assumption. The VBD-versus-Newton question is genuinely contested: VBD's authors found Newton better per iteration but slower in wall-clock, while expecting Newton to win for very stiff, high-resolution cases, and JGS2 reports VBD failing where subspace-Newton converges; for garments of inextensible woven cloth the evidence leans toward Newton-like convergence, but only on 3090-class tablecloth scenes. Whether OGC's guarantees survive a skinned, animated body is unresolved (the authors flag prescribed-motion penetration). The diagnosis of failure 1 (residual re-injection, contact active-set switching) is untested here, and no paper provides a "time to rest" metric for cloth. The bending fidelity problem has no published universal mesh-scaling fix, and wavelength-versus-element-size scaling was not sourced. Several key papers were not read or retrieved: Goldenthal's algorithm, Small Steps content, the AVBD paper body, Xian et al. 2019 and the 2025 Fast Galerkin multigrid paper, Volino and Magnenat-Thalmann 2006, Berthouzoz 2013, Baraff-Witkin-Kass, Harmon 2008, Wrinkle Meshes, and the SpongeCake fabric rendering line. Thickness, seam allowance and interfacing modelling, and CLO or Style3D seam internals, are undocumented in the located sources. Licences are unverified for AVBD demos, AASolver, StiffGIPC, BAL, AGIPC, libThinPlateShells, ContourCraft, GDSR, GarmentCodeData, Cycles, Mitsuba and Godot (assumed MIT). Rendering evidence is thin on chiffon translucency, Godot sheen support in 4.7, skin and eye shading, and M3 path-tracing speed. Several 2026-dated items in the notes (OGC-era follow-ups, AGIPC, YASPS, PAA, Gong, Mitsuba Metal, HyperBones) come from preprint pages or summaries and were read at abstract level, so treat their numbers as provisional.

## Conclusion

The most useful insight from the evidence is that the product's six failures collapse into three decisions. Rest, pinch flicker and stiff-mode swing are one problem (a non-converging step meeting switching contact), solved by convergence plus smooth contact, and the cheapest versions of both exist today in a form the team can add without new hardware. Gather quality is mostly a mesh-layout and bending-discretisation problem, which the published evidence says can be attacked before any real-time 1.5 cm solver exists. Fidelity is gated by measurement, which is why the regression suite ranks first.

What the evidence does not support is a quick leap to a CUDA-class GPU solver on an M3, or to a neural preview for arbitrary patterns. The honest plan is to measure first (an M3 baseline of the existing VBD experiment, a re-measure of Godot 4.7 on Metal), fix convergence and contact on the CPU path, and let the benchmark results, not the literature's RTX numbers, decide how much of the new core goes to Metal.
